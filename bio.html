Bio

I am a Research Scientist in the AI & Economics lab in Spotify’s Personalization organization. My research focuses on causal inference and data-driven decision-making, with an emphasis on practical problems in personalization, experimentation, and the design and evaluation of policies and algorithmic systems. I look for solutions that are simple to use and theoretically principled. My work has spanned digital health, online platforms, digital media, public policy, and algorithmic fairness. I am also interested in the interface between statistics, economics, and optimization.

My research has appeared in Marketing Letters, Eurosurveillance, and the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, and at machine learning conferences including KDD and MLHC. I have also presented my research and organized sessions at AIMS at COLM, CODE@MIT, NABE TEC, the American Causal Inference Conference, and INFORMS meetings.

At Spotify, I lead research and development on experimentation and evaluation for recommender systems, including how AI can augment traditional approaches. The methods and tools I have developed are used internally for product decision-making and run in production on the Spotify Homepage.

I received my PhD from ETH Zurich in 2024, advised by Stefan Feuerriegel and Florian von Wangenheim. During my doctorate, I visited the Operations, Information, and Technology group at Stanford GSB, hosted by Jann Spiess, and interned as a Machine Learning Research Scientist at Booking.com. I hold master’s and bachelor’s degrees in Statistics and Business & Economics from Lund University.

I was the first technical contributor and a program lead at Algorithm Audit, a Dutch NGO that provides independent assessments and advice on the ethical and fair use of algorithmic and AI systems. I contributed to policy reports and to an open-source tool for privacy-preserving bias evaluation. Before graduate school, I developed marketing mix models at GfK (acquired by NielsenIQ) and worked in performance marketing at Precis Digital.
